# Lead and crew

Written 2 September 2026. The shape is `lead-crew` in `src/core/shape.ts`.

## What it is for

The operator's own loop, made into a shape. Before Crosstalk they planned a
feature with a strong model in one app (the SPOC), then had it babysit a
weaker builder in another: the SPOC wrote the spec, told the builder what to
do, and checked in on a timer to see whether the builder had actually finished
or had stopped at a checkpoint nobody asked for and called it done. It worked,
and it cost nine to twelve hours per feature — most of it silence — and a turn
every twenty-five minutes to find out whether anything had happened.

`lead-crew` keeps the parts that worked and removes the timer.

## The shape

One **lead** (the `leader` role) and a crew of **builders** (`worker`s) that
the lead hires itself during the run. The launcher lays out only the lead;
the builder count starts at zero and the seat that has read the job decides
how many it needs.

| phase | who | leaves when |
| --- | --- | --- |
| plan | lead | the operator has answered a question the lead put to them; `docs/crosstalk/SPEC.md` exists and is committed; at least one builder is hired; the split is posted |
| build | everyone | no two seat branches touch the same file; **every task has been accepted by the lead** |
| verify | lead | the bug list is posted before anything is fixed |
| repair | lead | a clean run and a first-hand verification of the assembled build are posted |

Two of those gates are new and both are derived, not asserted:

- **`crew-hired`** reads the roster. A shape whose crew is hired at runtime
  cannot leave plan with nobody to build.
- **`tasks-accepted`** reads the task projection. It is met when there is at
  least one task and every task is `accepted` or `merged`. A builder's `done`
  moves its task to `submitted`; only the lead's `act({kind:"accept"})`
  moves it past. That is the mechanism behind "the lead keeps the builders
  in line": nothing a builder says about its own work counts until the lead
  has run it.

## Hiring

`act({kind:"hire", id, harness, model, effort})` → `POST /seats`. The lead or
the operator may call it; a builder may not. The daemon appends the seat to
`crosstalk.yaml`, runs `init --force` (worktree, brief, token, settings, as at
launch), reloads so the seat can authenticate, and spawns that one seat with
an opening turn that sends it to its inbox. The lead then `assign`s it a
task; the card is delivered to the seat's first `inbox()`.

Refusals, all mechanical:

- an id that is not a participant id, a harness that cannot be spawned, a
  role other than `worker`;
- an id already on the roster (`SEAT_EXISTS`);
- more than seven non-human seats (`SEAT_CAP`);
- **the spec exists and is not committed (`SPEC_UNCOMMITTED`).** A hired
  seat's worktree is cut from the main branch. A spec only the lead's working
  tree holds is a spec no builder can read — the seam bug, arriving as a
  file. The lead's brief says to commit before hiring; the daemon checks.

`act({kind:"release", id})` → `POST /seats/:id/stop` kills the seat's process
and nothing else. The worktree, the branch and any uncommitted work are left
alone, exactly as ending a run leaves them. The lead releases a builder once
its task is accepted, because a seat with nothing to do is a process the
operator is paying for.

## Nothing polls

The lead does not check on builders on a timer. A builder's `done` wakes the
lead with a `submitted` card; a `blocked` wakes it; a `reject` wakes the
builder. Between events every seat is parked on a long poll that costs
nothing.

The failure that made the operator poll — a builder that ended its turn with
the task open — is caught by the daemon instead (`src/daemon/watch.ts`):

1. A builder whose last presence report says its turn ended, holding a task
   in `assigned`/`acknowledged`/`in_progress`/`self_reviewed`, and silent for
   ten minutes, is sent one card in a side room with `@crosstalk`: finish it
   and `act done`, or say `blocked` and to whom. The card wakes it.
2. If it is still silent ten minutes after that, the lead is sent one card
   naming the seat and the task, with the three options: wait, reassign,
   release and re-hire.
3. A builder whose process has exited with a task open goes straight to the
   lead — nothing is there to read a nudge.
4. Symmetrically, a lead whose turn ended with a task sitting in `submitted`
   for ten minutes is reminded once per task.

Every episode is reported once. A seat that starts working again starts a
new episode. Presence comes from the Claude Code hooks `init` already writes,
and — for a Codex seat, which runs no hooks — from the turn process starting
and exiting.

## A Codex lead

The operator's planning model runs on Codex. Three things stood between that
and a seat that could lead a run, and all three were silent:

- **`codex exec` reads its prompt once and exits.** A wake had nowhere to go.
  The registry now declares `turnFormat: resume` for `codex-cli`: the first
  turn is `codex exec --json <job>`, whose first line names the thread, and
  every later turn is `codex exec … resume <thread> <turn>`. One process per
  turn, serialised, on one conversation. Verified against codex-cli 0.151.0.
- **The brief went to `AGENTS.local.md`.** Codex reads `AGENTS.md` and
  `AGENTS.override.md` and nothing else — the binary names both. The registry
  now declares `briefSuffix: override` and the brief lands where it is read.
- **MCP lived in `~/.codex/config.toml`,** which `init` refuses to write, so
  every Codex seat was briefed for the shell and told to run a `crosstalk`
  binary that is on nobody's PATH. A spawned Codex seat is now handed its
  registration as `-c mcp_servers.crosstalk.*` on its own command line,
  with its own token, and is briefed for MCP.

Also fixed on the way: `--effort` is not a `codex exec` flag, so a Codex seat
with an effort set died on argument parsing. Effort is now passed as
`-c model_reasoning_effort="…"`.

The spawn line runs Codex with `--sandbox workspace-write`, approvals pinned
to `never` (nobody is at that terminal to answer), and network on so the lead
can push and open pull requests.

## What the operator does

1. Start a run with shape `lead-crew`, one seat on `codex-cli` (or any
   supervisable harness), builders at 0, and the job in the box. Say in the
   job which harness and model the builders should be on, if you care.
2. Answer the lead's question when it appears on the board.
3. Read the hub when a card says so. Otherwise, nothing.

## What is not here

- No time box. The shape makes silence visible and cheap; it does not make a
  model faster. If a feature is too big for one run, the lead is the seat
  that should say so, in plan, and the operator's answer is a smaller job.
- No token accounting per seat. The harness knows; the daemon does not.
- A Codex seat has no mirrored terminal keyboard: the hub shows what it
  said and what it ran, not a pty. Steering it is a message on the board.
